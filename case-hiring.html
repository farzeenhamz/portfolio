01

Overview

Draft

Candidates often receive silence or generic rejections, and recruiters have no simple way to give structured feedback. This draft PRD covers a first slice: recruiters choose a structured reason when they reject an application, a clear note is drafted from that choice and approved by a human, and candidates receive it. Skills based profiles, ATS integrations and employer discovery are later options. The first product slice has not been selected by evidence yet, and this PRD is a working proposal.

02

Problem and evidence

Assumption
  • P1 Candidates frequently receive silence or generic rejections and cannot tell what to improve.
  • P2 Recruiters handle high volumes and have limited time to give feedback.
  • P3 Screening configurations may exclude relevant candidates (Hypothesis, to be investigated).
  • P4 A feedback system must never invent rejection reasons, and must separate employer decisions from independent analysis.

These come from my own experience in the India and UAE job markets. Several statistics from earlier research discussions are unverified and are not used here. A reported example about keyword mismatch in screening is also unverified and is not used.

03

Personas and jobs to be done

Assumption

Hypothetical personas based on assumptions, not interview data.

Candidate

An early career person or career changer who wants to know what to improve.

Recruiter or HR coordinator

At a small or mid sized employer, with high application volume and little time.

Jobs to be done

  • When I am rejected, I want to know what to improve so my next application is stronger.
  • When I reject many applicants, I want to give a clear, safe reason quickly.

Scope, in and out

Draft

In scope

  • Fixed reason code list (mandatory requirement not met, preferred qualification not shown, application incomplete, role filled or withdrawn, other)
  • Draft note
  • Human approval
  • Audit log
  • Candidate note view
  • Optional independent application insight, clearly labelled as independent

Out of scope

  • Candidate ranking or scores
  • Automatic rejection
  • ATS integrations
  • Employer directory
  • Scraping vacancies
  • Mentoring marketplace
  • Salary data

Non functional requirements

Draft
  • HNFR1 Meets WCAG AA.
  • HNFR2 Privacy by design, minimal data, retention limits to be set with legal advice.
  • HNFR3 Every note traceable to its selected code.
  • HNFR4 If the AI is unavailable, fall back to template text.
  • HNFR5 Fairness testing before any pilot, method to be defined.
08

Business rules

Draft
  • HBR1 Never disclose other applicants' information.
  • HBR2 No scores or ranks shown to candidates in the first slice.
  • HBR3 Employers choose per role whether to send feedback.
  • HBR4 Mandatory and preferred criteria must be recorded separately.
  • HBR5 Reasons are limited to the employer approved list.
09

User stories with acceptance criteria

Draft

Six stories for the first slice, each with written acceptance criteria.

  • As a recruiter, I pick a reason in one click so I can reject quickly.

    Given
    a recruiter reviewing an application marked for rejection
    When
    they select a reason code
    Then
    the system records the code and offers a drafted note

  • As a recruiter, I approve a drafted note so I stay in control.

    Given
    a drafted note
    When
    the recruiter edits or approves it
    Then
    only the approved text is sent and the approval is logged

  • As a candidate, I read what was missing so I can improve.

    Given
    a candidate who received a note
    When
    they open it
    Then
    they see the reason, the labelled sections and one suggested next step

  • As a candidate, I can ask for a correction if I think a reason is wrong.

    Given
    a candidate who disagrees with a reason
    When
    they submit a correction request
    Then
    the recruiter is notified and the request is recorded

  • As an employer admin, I set which reasons are allowed.

    Given
    an employer admin
    When
    they edit the allowed reason list for a role
    Then
    recruiters can choose only from that list

  • As a candidate, I request deletion of my data.

    Given
    a verified deletion request
    When
    it is processed
    Then
    the data is removed or anonymised and the request is logged
10

Edge cases and exceptions

Concept
  • Free text in other contains sensitive or discriminatory content, so it is flagged for review.
  • Candidate disputes a reason.
  • Role withdrawn after rejection.
  • Duplicate applications.
  • The draft contains an unsupported claim, so it is blocked.
11

AI requirements and human oversight

Concept
  • The model only rewrites structured inputs into clear language.
  • It never infers protected characteristics or hidden reasons.
  • A human reviews every note.
  • Output quality is judged on a sample set reviewed by humans. No results exist yet.
  • Model inputs and outputs are logged for audit.
12

Data, privacy and compliance

Concept

Data items

  • Application ID
  • Role
  • Reason code
  • Optional comment
  • Draft
  • Approved text
  • Timestamps

CV text is used only for the independent insight feature, with consent.

To research

  • UAE personal data protection law
  • India's DPDP framework
  • GDPR where relevant
  • Whether recruitment use falls under high risk rules in the EU AI Act
  • Employment discrimination law
  • Automated decision safeguards
  • Retention and deletion

Privacy lens

Design principles I would apply: collect only what is needed, use data only for the stated purpose, be transparent with the person, set retention limits, allow correction and deletion, and keep a human in charge of automated decisions that affect people. These are design principles, not legal advice. They must be checked against the UAE personal data protection law, India's DPDP framework and GDPR where relevant.

21

Reflection

Draft

In my own words, to be refined

So far I have reconstructed this idea from my own experience of job searching, mapped where recruitment can fail candidates, and narrowed a very large idea to one small slice. What I learned is that the hard question is why employers would take part, not what candidates want. I moved ranking and scores out of scope and made human approval mandatory, because a feedback tool that invents reasons would damage trust. Next I will interview job seekers and recruiters, scan competitors properly and test a clickable prototype. I do not yet know whether employers would adopt this or what legal limits apply.
