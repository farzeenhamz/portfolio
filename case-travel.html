01

Overview

Draft

Travel planning is spread across many apps, and travellers who need a visa can lose money by booking before approval. This draft PRD covers a first slice for one traveller corridor, with a working example of a traveller living in India visiting Switzerland. It offers budget first package discovery, a visa timeline with a tracker supplied by a partner agency, and a safer booking order. Financing, paid local guides, traveller matching and 24/7 emergency support are later options. The first product slice has not been selected by evidence yet.

02

Problem and evidence

Assumption
  • P1 Planning is fragmented across flights, stays, visas, insurance and local help (Assumption).
  • P2 Budget is hard to estimate because costs are calculated separately (Assumption).
  • P3 Booking before visa approval can cost money, and embassy rules and the order of steps differ by nationality and residence (Quick scan, see evidence log).
  • P4 Upfront cost limits international travel, and instalment options already exist on some platforms, so payment alone is not a differentiator (Hypothesis).
  • P5 Travellers may want trustworthy local help (Hypothesis, later).

Personas and jobs to be done

Assumption

Hypothetical personas based on assumptions, not interview data.

Solo traveller or solo parent

Needs a visa, wants a nature focused trip, has a set budget and wants less effort and more safety.

First time international traveller

Budget conscious and new to visa steps.

Jobs to be done

  • When I plan a trip that needs a visa, I want one clear plan that protects my money so I can book without fear.
  • When I set a budget, I want to see what is included and excluded so there are no surprises.

05

Scope, in and out

Draft

In scope

  • Inputs for passport, residence, dates, travellers, budget and preferences
  • One recommended package with alternatives on request
  • Price labels (estimate, live quote, confirmed)
  • Visa rule summary with source and date
  • Booking order guided by the visa timeline
  • Visa tracker showing information from a partner agency
  • Insurance check against the embassy minimum
  • Inclusions, exclusions and cancellation terms

Out of scope

  • Lending by the company
  • 24/7 emergency service
  • Same country traveller matching
  • Paid companion marketplace
  • Global coverage
  • An own booking engine

Non functional requirements

Draft
  • TNFR1 Passport and ID data encrypted and kept to a minimum.
  • TNFR2 Meets WCAG AA.
  • TNFR3 Partial booking failures across suppliers have defined handling and refunds.
  • TNFR4 Visa rule content shows a review date and is re checked on a schedule.
  • TNFR5 Response and availability targets to be defined.
08

Business rules

Draft
  • TBR1 The app guides and prepares, the embassy decides.
  • TBR2 AI never invents availability, prices or visa requirements.
  • TBR3 Every price carries a type label.
  • TBR4 Non refundable bookings follow visa approval by default.
  • TBR5 Every visa statement shows source and date.

10

Edge cases and exceptions

Concept
  • Price changes during checkout.
  • A hotel becomes unavailable after the flight is booked.
  • The visa is refused after refundable holds.
  • Appointment slots are unavailable.
  • A traveller cancels a financed package.
  • A supplier gives inaccurate information.
  • The AI recommends an attraction that is closed.
11

AI requirements and human oversight

Concept
  • AI works only as a recommendation layer.
  • It reads verified inventory and verified visa content and never creates availability, prices or rules.
  • Visa content is human reviewed.
  • Output is checked against live data on a sample of itineraries. No results exist yet.
12

Data, privacy and compliance

Concept

Data includes passport details, nationality, residence, dates and preferences including dietary needs, handled as sensitive.

To research

  • Travel agency and tour operator licensing
  • Package travel rules
  • Consumer protection
  • Refund obligations
  • Payment and lending regulation
  • Insurance distribution
  • Guide licensing
  • Data protection including cross border transfers
  • Marketplace liability

Privacy lens

Design principles I would apply: collect only what is needed, use data only for the stated purpose, be transparent with the person, set retention limits, allow correction and deletion, and keep a human in charge of automated decisions that affect people. These are design principles, not legal advice. They must be checked against the UAE personal data protection law, India's DPDP framework and GDPR where relevant.

21

Reflection

Draft

In my own words, to be refined

So far I have broken a large travel marketplace idea into one narrow problem: booking in the wrong order when a visa is needed. I learned that most single features already exist, so the question is whether the combination is useful and defensible. I moved payments, paid guides, traveller matching and emergency support to later because they bring regulation, safety and cost that I cannot test yet. Next I will verify the visa rules for one corridor, check competitors properly and interview travellers. I do not yet know whether travellers want this from one provider or which partners would work with it.
